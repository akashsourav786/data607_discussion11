Discussion title: Steam's Interactive Recommender: Beyond Past Playtime

For Discussion 11, I analyzed Steam's Interactive Recommender from both the player's and Valve's organizational perspectives. Valve's published explanation describes a neural model trained on gameplay histories, with controls for exploring popular or niche games, release dates, and tags. I distinguish this documented description from my own inferences and acknowledge that I did not inspect signed-in recommendations.

My main argument is that past behavior does not fully explain what someone wants next. A player with hundreds of hours in a multiplayer game may currently want a short, single-player experience. I recommend clearer goal-setting, explanations of exclusion controls, coordinated discovery for sparse histories, and evaluation of satisfaction after purchase.

The GitHub repository linked with this post contains my Quarto notebook and generated Markdown report.

Question for discussion: Should a game recommender prioritize demonstrated past behavior or a player's stated goal when the two point in different directions?
